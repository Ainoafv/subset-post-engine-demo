Turning the Post Engineering Brain into a working system

Technical proposal for Subset · prepared by Ainoa F., AI & Data Engineer · working demo of one slice

Summary. Treat the Brain as a grammar with a type system: elements, attributes and rules become a versioned, machine-checkable specification. Generation runs in stages under that spec, so the model only fills slots it is allowed to fill. Quality is split in two, as you described: deterministic validators for everything objectively testable (this is how 99.99% construction compliance is reached and measured) and independent, evidence-grounded reviewers for buyer relevance, expertise and writing quality, calibrated against your expert-reviewed examples. Every rule change runs a regression suite before it ships. Start with a paid technical engagement of ~2 weeks that delivers a gap report on the Brain, an executable spec for one or two post types and a working prototype.

The specification sits beside the pipeline, not inside prompts:

  • Schemas for each element and sub-element (Buyer DNA attributes, information types, post/sentence/clause types, word categories), with typed attributes and explicit dependencies.
  • Rules as data: constraints, formulas, exceptions and precedence in versioned config files (YAML in Git, or a small admin UI later). Your team edits rules; the engine reads them. Routine changes need no code.
  • Gold set: expert-approved posts and known-bad cases, each tagged with the rules it exercises. It runs in CI on every rule or prompt change, and the report shows exactly what moved.

Claims are true and supportedReviewer must map every claim to a source passage; unmapped claims are flagged; numbers must carry a citation (checked in code)Unsupported-claim rate; precision/recall of the reviewer against expert labels

Reviewers are kept independent from generation (separate prompts, optionally a different model) and are themselves evaluated: a reviewer that disagrees with your experts is a bug to fix, not a score to trust.

3. What I expect to find in the Brain (gap review)

From the posting, these are the questions I'd expect the first review to answer. Each becomes either a rule, an exception or an explicit "human decides".

  • Precedence: when two rules conflict (e.g. a buyer attribute pushes one information type, a quality standard limits it), which wins?
  • Importance formula: how exactly does each of the 20 Buyer DNA attributes weight information selection? Are weights fixed, per buyer, or per post type?
  • Exceptions: post types or buyers where a construction rule does not apply.
  • Testability: which quality standards are objectively testable (→ validators) and which need judgment (→ reviewers with a rubric)?
  • Evidence granularity: what counts as support for a claim, and how fine-grained client expertise sources need to be.
  • Versioning: who can change which rules, and how a change is approved before it reaches production.
